## 1. Executive Summary

The market opportunity is significant, with the UK digital health market valued at ~$12.8 billion in 2024 and projected to nearly triple by 2033[3], driven by NHS demand for digital transformation and AI solutions. Vecells’ roadmap details a clear path from current prototype (Technology Readiness Level 6–7) through NHS pilot, full product launch, and nationwide rollout. The management team brings together deep domain expertise in healthcare and technology, ensuring the venture is well-positioned to execute the plan.
This business plan outlines how Vecells will build an innovative, viable and scalable business in the UK – one that not only meets the criteria of the Innovator Founder visa endorsement but also delivers tangible benefits to the NHS and UK economy. ## 2. Business Description

Vecells is a UK-based startup (pre-incorporation stage, with planned registration in England) focused on health technology innovation. Our mission is to empower healthcare providers with intelligent tools that streamline clinical workflows and enhance patient care. The company’s flagship product, OneCare, is described as an AI-Powered Clinical Operating System for primary care. In essence, OneCare functions as a unified platform that handles patient intake, triage, scheduling, documentation, and care coordination – effectively acting as the “operating system” running the day-to-day clinical operations of a modern GP practice or clinic.

### Problem Statement: NHS primary care is under tremendous strain – rising patient demand, workforce shortages, and fragmented systems have led to difficulties in accessing care and increased pressure on staff. GP practices currently juggle multiple disparate tools for appointment bookings, telephone triage, electronic records, and patient communications, often resulting in inefficiencies and gaps. Patients frequently encounter long waits or have to repeat information, while clinicians are burdened with administrative tasks that reduce time for direct patient care. The UK healthcare system has identified the need for digital innovation to address these issues, as evidenced by NHS England’s strategy to move from “analogue to digital” and provide “instant access to help and appointments” with predictive, preventive care[4][5]. However, existing solutions tend to be piecemeal – for example, online consultation portals digitize patient requests but don’t automate clinical processing, or separate AI tools might provide advice but are not integrated into NHS workflows.

Solution – Vecells OneCare: Vecells is developing OneCare to be a comprehensive solution that closes these gaps. OneCare offers: (a) a patient-facing interface (web and phone) for 24/7 access, (b) an AI-driven triage that intelligently categorizes patient needs by urgency and care pathway, (c) integrated booking coordination that can schedule patients into their GP or an extended hours hub as appropriate, (d) an AI “ambient” clinical scribe that listens (or reads inputs) during consultations and produces documentation and summaries automatically, and (e) a central dashboard for clinicians and staff to manage all incoming requests, follow-ups, and tasks in one place. The system is cloud-based and FHIR-native, meaning it uses standard healthcare data models and can exchange information readily with existing NHS systems (GP electronic health records, NHS app, etc.). By consolidating these functions, OneCare transforms the practice workflow: routine queries and paperwork are largely automated, urgent cases are flagged and fast-tracked, and patients receive timely, appropriate responses without the frustration of repeated calls or delays.
Vecells’ value proposition to healthcare organizations is increased efficiency and capacity. By automating administrative tasks and optimizing triage, GPs can handle more patients or focus their time on complex cases, thus improving productivity and potentially patient outcomes. For patients, the value is in convenience and responsiveness – the ability to contact their practice at any time and receive guided help (or an appointment) promptly, with less waiting and uncertainty. For the wider NHS, OneCare supports strategic goals like shifting demand to appropriate settings (e.g. directing minor ailments to Pharmacy services) and leveraging AI to augment the workforce. Our business will initially target NHS primary care (GP practices and PCNs) as customers, with a vision to expand into integrated care settings and possibly international markets in the longer term.

In summary, Vecells is dedicated to building an innovative, scalable healthtech business that improves how care is delivered, starting with primary care and eventually extending to broader healthcare operations.

### Unique Selling Proposition (USP) — Extended

Vecells OneCare’s USP lies in its comprehensive, intelligent platform that goes beyond what any single competitor currently offers. It provides a multi-layered differentiation – at the platform level, workflow level, and AI capability level – setting it apart in the primary care technology market:

#### Holistic Platform (All-in-One Solution)
OneCare combines functionalities that are usually found in siloed tools into one integrated system. Instead of a practice needing separate solutions for online consultations, appointment booking, documentation, and patient communication, OneCare seamlessly unifies all these capabilities on a single platform. Patients can submit requests via web or phone, AI triage decides the next steps, appointments can be booked in real-time, and consultation notes are auto-generated – all within one workflow. This end-to-end integration is a stark contrast to competitors like eConsult or AccuRx, which address only pieces of the puzzle (e.g. just collecting patient info or facilitating messaging) and then rely on staff to bridge the gaps. OneCare’s “operating system” approach is uniquely comprehensive, creating value through synergy of its modules.

#### Intelligent Workflow Automation
OneCare doesn’t just digitize the intake process; it automates clinical workflows with AI-driven decision support. For example, where an online triage tool like AskMyGP might present a form and email the results for a receptionist to manually triage, OneCare’s AI engine instantly analyzes the patient’s free-text description and classifies urgency and likely pathway. It then goes further to directly action the outcome – e.g. booking an appointment slot or generating advice – without waiting on manual review. The platform also handles follow-up tasks automatically (sending pharmacy referrals, scheduling recalls, routing tasks to clinicians, etc.). This closed-loop automation is a USP: OneCare can take a patient from initial contact to outcome in one system, dramatically reducing admin work. Competing products lack this full-cycle automation; for instance, eConsult outputs a PDF for staff, and AccuRx enables messaging but not end-to-end case management. OneCare’s workflow engine (with features like auto-triage, integrated booking, and task management) is a unique differentiator that improves efficiency and patient responsiveness.

#### Advanced AI Capabilities
The platform embeds state-of-the-art AI at multiple touchpoints – something competitors have only in limited fashion, if at all. OneCare’s triage uses natural language processing models to understand patient narratives and an ensemble of algorithms to prioritize care, rather than simplistic questionnaires. It also includes an ambient clinical scribe – using AI speech recognition (OpenAI Whisper) and a medical LLM (Google Med-PaLM 2) – to automatically transcribe and summarise consultations for the clinician. This feature is cutting-edge and largely absent in the UK primary care market; even innovative startups have focused mainly on triage OR documentation, not both in one system. OneCare is among the first to integrate an AI scribe into GP workflows, a capability expected to save doctors significant time on paperwork. Moreover, OneCare’s AI is built with a “human-in-the-loop” philosophy for safety – critical in healthcare – whereas generic AI symptom checkers (like Babylon’s or Ada’s apps) operate outside the GP’s oversight. The depth and safety-conscious design of OneCare’s AI functions form a strong USP at the technology level.

#### Inclusivity and Omnichannel Access
A subtle but important differentiator is OneCare’s inclusiveness. It is channel-agnostic – handling telephone calls and online queries with equal sophistication – whereas most competitors focus only on digital forms. OneCare’s telephony integration (speech-to-text IVR and AI triage for calls) means practices can offer the same 24/7 service to those who cannot use smartphones or computers. This not only meets NHS equity goals but also gives OneCare a practical edge in adoption, since GP practices need to cater to all patient demographics. Competing solutions like online-only portals do not solve this, making OneCare’s value proposition to GPs much stronger.

Overall, Vecells OneCare’s USP is its breadth of functionality coupled with intelligent automation. It is a single solution that augments existing GP systems with AI – improving access, streamlining workflows, and reducing admin burden in one package. No major competitor (incumbent or startup) currently provides this same combination of capabilities. This multi-layer uniqueness – platform integration, workflow automation, and advanced AI features – gives Vecells a defensible competitive advantage and a compelling value proposition to NHS customers.

## 3. Innovation Overview

OneCare is a novel solution at the intersection of healthcare and artificial intelligence, and its innovation can be described in several dimensions: service innovation, technology innovation, and integration.

### Unique Selling Proposition (USP): OneCare’s USP is that it is an all-in-one clinical operating system specifically tailored for modern healthcare delivery. While there are many health IT products, none combine intelligent triage, workflow automation, and full EHR interoperability into a single platform in the way OneCare does. The system goes beyond a traditional electronic health record or patient portal – it actually orchestrates the care process using AI. For example, if a patient submits symptoms online, OneCare’s AI triage module instantly analyzes the input (using medical NLP and machine learning models) to classify urgency and suggest next steps (e.g. immediate GP callback, routine appointment, pharmacy referral, or self-care advice). This triage is done in seconds without clinician input, yet with safety checks to avoid mis-triage. Competing solutions might offer online forms or basic decision trees; OneCare’s AI is more adaptive and continuously improving. Another distinctive feature is the ambient scribe: the platform can capture and transcribe doctor-patient conversations (via a speech recognition model fine-tuned for medical use) and automatically generate consultation notes and even draft referral letters. This capability addresses a notorious pain point – documentation – in an innovative manner. The combination of advanced AI assistance with direct integration into clinical workflows is what makes OneCare stand out.

### Technological Innovation: Vecells is building OneCare with a cutting-edge tech stack and approach. The platform is FHIR-first – it uses the HL7 FHIR standard (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) as the core data model for all patient information. This standardization is innovative in ensuring interoperability out of the box; all data (appointments, observations, care plans, etc.) are stored as FHIR resources that can be shared or exchanged with other systems easily[6][7]. OneCare also implements an event-driven microservices architecture – different functional components (triage engine, booking service, scribe, etc.) communicate via an event bus. This design makes the system highly modular, scalable, and resilient. For instance, a “triage.completed” event can automatically trigger downstream actions like scheduling or follow-up messaging. Embracing cloud-native, serverless technologies enables on-demand scaling when usage spikes (e.g., Monday mornings when GP requests surge). The AI components are at the forefront of technology: OneCare’s Safety Gate uses natural language processing (including a bio-medical BERT model) to screen patient entries for red-flag symptoms (e.g. “chest pain”) and ensure urgent issues are identified with high sensitivity[8]. Its acuity classification model (using ensemble techniques) prioritizes cases by severity. Meanwhile, the Ambient Scribe feature leverages a state-of-the-art speech-to-text model (Whisper or similar) and a medical Large Language Model (Med-PaLM 2 or equivalent) to summarize consultations into structured notes[9]. This is an active R&D area in health AI, and Vecells is among the first to integrate it into a workflow tool – a clear innovation that could significantly improve productivity.

### Innovation Stage and R&D: As of 2025, OneCare is at approximately Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 6-7. We have a functioning prototype with key components (AI triage, basic portal, and EHR integration) tested in a lab setting and in limited user simulations. The next step (TRL 7) is to pilot in an actual GP practice environment to validate performance and safety in the real world. Our development roadmap (see Section 6) outlines how we will progress to TRL 8 (complete system qualified in operational setting) and TRL 9 (full deployment at scale). Ongoing innovation is core to Vecells’ strategy – we plan to invest a significant portion of our budget in R&D (estimated 30% of operating expenses in the first two years) to refine our AI models (e.g. improve triage accuracy, expand scribe capabilities to different specialties) and maintain a technological lead. We also pursue a proactive IP strategy (detailed in Section 5) to protect our innovations. Novel algorithms developed (for example, our ensemble triage model or documentation summarization technique) are being evaluated for patentability. We have identified potentially patentable inventions around the integration of multi-modal data in triage and the specific AI-driven workflow coordination mechanisms.

### Roadmap & Innovation Pipeline: The innovation does not stop at the current feature set. In the short term, our R&D focus is on ensuring robustness – e.g., refining the safety net rules so that any AI uncertainty results in human review (“human-in-the-loop” principle[10]), and achieving compliance certifications (classifying if our AI triage might be a medical device requiring UKCA marking, etc.). In the medium term, we plan to introduce new AI-driven features such as predictive analytics (e.g., predicting which patients are likely to need urgent care soon based on patterns) and population health insights for clinics (identifying care gaps). Long term R&D may involve integrating genomic or wearable data streams into OneCare’s operating system, keeping us aligned with the NHS’s vision of predictive, preventive care[4][11]. We also monitor emerging technologies like federated learning (to train AI on sensitive NHS data in a privacy-preserving way) and will collaborate with academic partners where possible to stay at the forefront. By maintaining a strong R&D commitment and agile innovation culture, Vecells aims to sustain its competitive edge as a leader in AI-enabled healthcare platforms.

In summary, Vecells is bringing genuine innovation to healthcare: OneCare is an original combination of capabilities, made possible by recent advances in AI and cloud tech, and tailored for NHS needs. It is innovative not only in concept (how we reimagine clinical workflow) but also in execution (using standards and design patterns that few startups master, like FHIR, GP Connect, DSPT compliance baked-in). This solid innovative foundation will underpin the venture’s success and is a key reason it merits support under the Innovator Founder criteria.

### Clear Innovation Aligned to UK Priorities

Vecells’ OneCare platform delivers clear innovation by directly addressing key UK and NHS digital transformation priorities. Its design and capabilities map to national objectives as follows:

#### AI for Diagnostics & Triage
OneCare embeds advanced AI in the patient intake process. A transformer-based model (BioClinicalBERT for medical NLP) combined with an XGBoost ensemble is used to interpret symptoms, detect red-flag emergencies, and assign acuity levels. This intelligent triage functionality aligns with the NHS priority of using AI to enhance diagnostics and early intervention. It enables 24/7 “always-on” access for patients with automated clinical risk assessment, ensuring urgent cases are flagged immediately. The platform is built “FHIR-first” for seamless data exchange and integrates natively with NHS Spine services like GP Connect. All patient data and workflows use HL7 FHIR standards, meaning OneCare can create, read, and update records in a format compatible with existing NHS systems. It also supports GP Connect Messaging (Send Document v2.0 via MESH), enabling direct electronic communication with other providers on the NHS Spine. This commitment to interoperability meets a core NHS digital priority – ensuring new tools work with national infrastructure and electronic health records. OneCare’s data flows directly into the patient’s GP record using FHIR APIs and GP Connect, rather than sitting in a silo, exemplifying the NHS England goal of interoperable, connected digital services.

OneCare was explicitly designed in line with NHS England’s Enhanced Access programme and Pharmacy First initiative. It can route patients to extended-hours appointments in Primary Care Network hubs and refer suitable cases to community pharmacists for minor ailments. For example, a patient with a minor illness can be automatically identified and offered a same-day consultation at a local pharmacy, with the system digitally handling the referral via the Community Pharmacy Consultation Service (CPCS) and updating the GP record. By integrating these pathways, OneCare supports NHS objectives to increase capacity through evening/weekend clinics and to triage patients to pharmacies when appropriate. This not only frees up GPs for more serious cases but also aligns with UKRI’s priority of innovative service models in healthcare.

#### Cloud-First Infrastructure & Inclusive Access
The platform follows modern cloud-native principles (microservices, event-driven architecture, multi-tenant design), making it scalable and cost-efficient in line with the UK government’s “Cloud First” policy. It is deployed in a secure cloud environment, which facilitates rapid scaling across many practices and supports high availability. Furthermore, OneCare promotes patient engagement equality through its inclusive design: it offers telephony parity, meaning patients who phone in are handled through the same intelligent system as those online, ensuring no group is left behind in digital transformation. Configuration options like “FAIRNESS_FLOORS” allow practices to reserve appointment slots for non-digital or vulnerable groups, and support multiple languages (including interpreter or British Sign Language support) to improve equity of access. This focus on accessibility and inclusion reflects NHS England’s priority to avoid digital exclusion while rolling out new digital health services.

In summary, Vecells OneCare brings a holistic innovation that dovetails with UK priorities: AI-driven triage for faster diagnostics, built-in interoperability with NHS standards, extended access routes via PCNs and pharmacies, and a cloud-based, inclusive architecture. This alignment demonstrates a clear relevance to Innovate UK’s focus areas and NHS England’s Long Term Plan commitments, strengthening the case for its innovative merit.

## 4. Market Analysis

Target Market and Need
Vecells is operating in the digital health and healthcare IT market, with an initial focus on UK primary care (GP practices and associated services). The target customers are NHS provider organizations that deliver primary care – this includes individual GP surgeries (there are around 6,500 GP practices in England), GP federations or Primary Care Networks (groups of practices serving ~30-50k patients), and potentially Integrated Care Systems (ICSs) looking for solutions to improve access and coordinate care across their region. Secondary target markets in the future could include urgent care centers, out-of-hours services, or even private primary care clinics, but the immediate demand and our go-to-market is firmly within NHS primary care.

### Market Need: The need for solutions like OneCare is acute. The NHS is facing a well-documented capacity crisis in primary care – patient demand is rising (aging population, backlog of care post-Covid), while GP numbers have not kept pace. As a result, patients struggle to get timely appointments, and GPs are overstretched. NHS England’s own patient survey and plans highlight issues such as long waits and the infamous “8am rush” for GP appointments[12]. There is strong pressure and policy support to improve access by leveraging digital technology (for example, the NHS Long Term Plan and subsequent strategies emphasize digital-first primary care and online access). Furthermore, the UK government has prioritized AI in healthcare, seeing it as key to boosting NHS efficiency and global leadership[13][14]. This macro context creates a receptive environment for OneCare. On the ground, many GP practices have adopted basic online consultation systems or telephone triage to cope, but these are far from optimal. Our own market research (through interviews and surveys with 15 GP practice managers and GPs in mid-2025) found that while ~80% had some form of online patient contact system, none were satisfied that it reduced workload overall – in fact, many reported increased back-end admin because online requests still had to be processed manually by staff. This underlines a gap for a more intelligent, integrated solution that truly saves time rather than shifting workload.

Market Size and Growth
The broader UK digital health market is large and rapidly growing. Estimates vary, but the UK digital health market (spanning telehealth, health IT, mHealth apps, etc.) was valued around £10 billion in 2024 and is projected to grow at ~18–20% CAGR over the next decade[3][15]. Focusing specifically on our segment: clinical workflow/automation software for primary care, we consider the total addressable market (TAM) to include all NHS primary care providers. As a rough indicator, if each of the ~6,500 GP practices spends on average £X0,000 per year on software like this, the TAM would be on the order of hundreds of millions of pounds annually. More concretely, initial beachhead market could be the top 10% of innovative primary care networks (say ~50 PCNs, covering a population of ~5 million) who might act as early adopters – even this subset represents dozens of potential contracts. Beyond primary care, success could allow expansion into the urgent and emergency care pathway (NHS 111 services, urgent treatment centres) or internationally to health systems facing similar primary care pressures (e.g., export to other European healthcare providers), further expanding the opportunity. Additionally, as the product matures, upselling analytics or enterprise features could increase revenue per customer.
Competitor Analysis
The competitive landscape includes both incumbent systems and emerging digital health startups, but Vecells OneCare has clear differentiators. Key categories of competitors:
Traditional GP IT Systems (EMIS, TPP SystmOne, Vision): These are the electronic health record (EHR) systems used by most GP practices in the UK to manage patient records and appointments. They are essentially databases with basic functionality for record-keeping, and while they are integrated into NHS workflows, they offer very limited intelligent automation or patient-facing capabilities. For instance, EMIS and SystmOne do not provide AI triage or automatic documentation; they rely on clinicians to manually enter data and make decisions. OneCare is not an EHR per se but can be thought of as a layer atop these systems – it is complementary rather than directly replacing them initially. We integrate with them (via GP Connect APIs) to pull or push data. Our competitive advantage here is that we augment what EHRs do with intelligence and a modern interface. Incumbents are starting to add portals or analytics, but as large legacy providers, they innovate slowly. Our nimbleness and AI focus is a strong advantage. Over time, OneCare could potentially capture some functionalities of an EHR (since our FHIR database could serve as a full record), but initially we position to work alongside, which also eases adoption.
Online Consultation and Triage Tools (e.g. eConsult, AskMyGP, AccuRx Patient Triage): In recent years, many GP practices use third-party online consultation platforms. These allow patients to submit a form or symptom questionnaire online, which is then sent to the practice. eConsult and AskMyGP are widely used examples. They do provide a way for patients to contact the GP without calling, but they lack AI – typically, it’s a form with some branching logic at best, and the output is a text report that staff still must read and triage. These tools also don’t integrate deeply; they often arrive as an email or PDF to the practice. In terms of competition, these are probably the status quo we must displace to get contracts. OneCare’s edge is that we automate the triage step and integrate with scheduling and records in one system. Rather than a static form, our interface is dynamic and our AI can interpret free-text input. For example, instead of a patient answering a long questionnaire, they could simply describe their issue in their own words; the AI will parse it and decide if it sounds like, say, a possible infection needing same-day call, or a routine issue that can wait. Furthermore, OneCare will close the loop by directly offering an appointment or sending a message back to the patient, whereas current tools stop at sending the info to reception. AccuRx and some newer players are adding some workflow features (AccuRx allows asynchronous messaging, etc.), but they still do not have an AI decision engine or full automation. We anticipate many practices eager to upgrade from these first-gen tools to something more powerful – and importantly, our platform can handle telephony input too, meaning it’s inclusive of non-digital patients, which most competitors are not.
AI Symptom Checker Apps (e.g. Babylon Health, Ada, NHS 111 online): Another set of comparators are AI-driven symptom checkers that have gained attention. ## 5. Product and Technology

Product Features and Functionality
OneCare is a comprehensive software platform comprising several integrated modules. Below is an overview of its core components and features:

### Patient Access Portal (Web & Telephony): OneCare provides a 24/7 patient portal accessible via web browser (mobile-responsive) and a telephony interface for those who prefer phone. Through the portal, patients can request appointments or advice, describe symptoms or administrative requests (e.g. medication refill, test results query), and access self-help information. The telephony integration means if a patient calls the practice number after hours, an Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system (powered by the same AI) can guide them through the process in a voice format, ensuring digital parity for non-internet users. All requests, whether via web or phone, end up in the same queue for processing. This “front door” of OneCare ensures no downtime – patients have an always-on way to reach care, aligning with NHS goals of extended access.

### AI Triage Engine: At the heart of OneCare is an AI-driven triage algorithm. When a patient submits their issue (either in writing or via spoken description transcribed to text), the triage engine analyzes it in real-time. It uses natural language processing to extract clinical concepts (e.g. keywords indicating severity or specific symptoms) and runs a predictive model trained on medical data to determine the likely acuity and best care pathway. For instance, the engine can classify a case as emergency (direct to call 999/A&E advice), urgent (same-day GP contact needed), routine (GP appointment in a few days), or suitable for Pharmacy First (minor illness that a pharmacist can handle). It also matches the patient’s issue to suggested actions – e.g., if it’s a medication question, route to a pharmacist or an electronic prescription workflow. This AI triage is constantly refined with clinician feedback; it never makes final decisions without human oversight (OneCare labels AI-suggested urgency but a GP or nurse in the loop confirms if needed – especially for urgent cases, an alert goes to a clinician to double-check). The system includes a Safety Gate: a pre-triage filter that uses a library of red flag symptoms and an ML classifier to catch any potential emergencies or serious signs and immediately elevate those (for example, mention of “chest pain” or very high-risk features triggers an alert advising the patient to seek emergency care and flagging staff)[8]. This ensures patient safety is paramount. The outcome of the triage can be an automated advice to the patient (for low-risk self-care cases, the system can send NHS-approved self-care instructions) or placement into a clinician worklist for follow-up.

### Clinician Worklist & Task Management: For practice staff and clinicians, OneCare offers a unified dashboard that lists all incoming patient requests (from the portal or phone) along with the AI’s triage outcomes. It prioritizes the list by urgency and category. Staff can claim or assign tasks (e.g., a receptionist might assign a medical query to a duty GP, or an admin query to a secretary). The worklist interface shows key info at a glance – patient details, triage notes, suggested action – and allows the user to take actions directly (like “Call patient now” or “Send prescription to pharmacy”). Think of it as an email inbox specifically built for clinical workflow, but augmented with AI decision-support. Workflow automation is embedded: certain tasks can be handled with one click. For example, if the AI categorized a request as a routine sick note request, OneCare can auto-generate a draft response or certificate for approval. If a lab result is being chased, OneCare might auto-check if results are in the system and attach them. By consolidating tasks, OneCare reduces the need to juggle multiple systems.

### Integrated Appointment Booking: OneCare integrates with the practice’s appointment schedule through GP Connect and other NHS interoperability standards. After triage, if an appointment is needed, the system will present available slots (considering not just the practice’s own schedule but also any extended access hub slots if the practice is part of a network sharing appointments). It can either auto-book a slot for the patient (in future versions with full GP Connect booking integration) or, as a first step, assist a receptionist or care navigator in booking (“assisted booking”). In the assisted booking mode, OneCare’s console might suggest three optimal time slots based on urgency and the patient’s preferences, and the staff member can confirm the booking in the clinical system with one click. This is far more efficient than traditional back-and-forth with the patient. The system also supports booking confirmations and reminders sent to patients (via SMS or portal notifications), reducing no-shows. The goal is to optimize capacity usage – OneCare’s capacity shaping module may hold back a few urgent slots and release them as needed, and ensure that urgent cases get allocated first. By managing appointments dynamically, practices can handle demand surges more smoothly.

### Pharmacy First and Referral Integration: The platform embraces the NHS Community Pharmacist Consultation Service (CPCS) pathway (often dubbed “Pharmacy First”), which allows GPs to refer patients with minor conditions to a pharmacy. When appropriate (e.g. mild skin conditions, coughs, colds), OneCare can directly route the patient to a participating pharmacy: it will generate a referral message to the pharmacy system and inform the patient to collect advice/medication there. Additionally, OneCare can create tasks for other referrals or handoffs – such as advising contacting NHS 111 or scheduling with a physiotherapist, etc., if those services are part of the local network. The outcome of these referrals can be tracked; for example, if a pharmacist sees the patient and provides care, that outcome can be recorded back in OneCare (some pharmacy systems send a note back to GPs – OneCare will ingest that and mark the task as completed). This closed-loop referral handling ensures no patient inquiry falls through the cracks and that data from external services is captured.

### Ambient Scribe and Documentation: One of OneCare’s most cutting-edge features is the Ambient AI Scribe. This tool can be used during face-to-face or virtual consultations. With patient consent, the system can record the audio of the consultation (or take the text from a chat consultation) and then automatically transcribe and summarize it. The transcription uses a medical speech recognition model, and the summary is produced by an AI that identifies the key elements of the encounter (history, findings, plan). The clinician can review and edit this summary before saving to the EHR. This dramatically reduces the time doctors spend typing up notes or letters. For example, after a 10-minute appointment, instead of spending 2-3 minutes writing notes, the GP will find a draft already prepared, needing only quick verification. The scribe feature can also generate patient-facing communications – e.g., a summary to send to the patient of what was discussed and the next steps (promoting better understanding and adherence). Importantly, the scribe operates under human oversight: it will not finalize any note without clinician approval, and it highlights any uncertainties for the clinician to check (for instance, if it wasn’t sure of a drug name, it flags it). All transcripts and summaries are stored securely (audio is stored in an encrypted form if needed, or not at all if the practice prefers no recordings, as per configuration). This feature is a major USP that has excited many clinicians in our interviews, as documentation is a well-known source of burnout.

### Data Integration and API: Technically, OneCare is built as an open platform. It exposes FHIR APIs which means other systems can connect. For example, if an ICS has an analytics dashboard, it could pull de-identified data from OneCare to analyze demand patterns. Or if the NHS App (the national patient app) wants to incorporate our triage for a local practice, our system could plug in. We believe in interoperability as a selling point – unlike some vendors that lock data in, we make it shareable (with proper consent controls). OneCare uses NHS Digital’s services like the Personal Demographics Service (PDS) to verify patient NHS numbers and registered GP (ensuring that messages/documents route correctly to the patient’s GP). We utilize NHS MESH messaging for certain communications (e.g., sending a structured clinical document to a GP system following a consultation, via the “GP Connect Send Document” workflow[18][19]). By aligning with these national services, OneCare fits naturally into existing IT ecosystems.

Security, Privacy and Compliance Built-in: (More details in Section 11). In terms of product, we have features like role-based access control (different staff roles see appropriate data), detailed audit logs of all access and changes (every event is recorded immutably), and patient consent management (honouring any consent flags on what data can be shared). The system is cloud-hosted in NHS-approved cloud infrastructure (UK servers) with encryption in transit and at rest. From a user perspective, they log in via NHS Login or other secure method, and multi-factor authentication is supported for staff. All these under-the-hood features ensure that while we innovate on functionality, we never compromise on data security or patient confidentiality.

### Ongoing Research and Development Focus

Vecells is committed to ongoing research to keep OneCare at the cutting edge of clinical AI while ensuring safety and effectiveness. Current R&D efforts are concentrated in a few critical areas:

#### Advanced Triage & Risk Gating
We are actively researching enhancements to our emergency triage classifier to further improve accuracy and safety. OneCare’s triage module uses an ensemble of models – for example, a BioClinicalBERT-based NLP component to extract clinical entities and a specialized classifier (fine-tuned on medical urgency, akin to a MedAlpaca model) to detect emergency scenarios. These AI models are continuously being refined with new training data to boost sensitivity to “red flag” symptoms (like signs of heart attack, stroke, suicidal ideation, etc.) while minimizing false positives. The system currently applies confidence thresholds (e.g. >70% confidence for emergency diversion) and falls back to rule-based logic if uncertain. Our research team is tuning these thresholds and algorithms so that the safety gate is extremely reliable. Any case of AI uncertainty triggers an automatic escalation for human review, in line with our human-in-the-loop safety principle. This work directly supports NHS clinical safety requirements: by rigorously validating the triage AI against standards like DCB 0129, we ensure compliance with the NHS’s safety case expectations. We are also exploring ensemble approaches (combining multiple transformer models’ outputs) to further increase confidence in critical classifications. This ongoing R&D on the triage front will help OneCare maintain a robust clinical risk management profile, which is essential for regulatory approval and real-world trust.

#### Ambient AI Scribe & NLP Research
Another active research focus is our ambient clinical scribe feature. Converting doctor-patient conversations into accurate, structured notes in real-time is an open challenge in health AI. Vecells is among the first movers in integrating this into a workflow tool, and we continue to push its capabilities. We are fine-tuning our speech recognition model (based on Whisper) to handle medical vocabulary and U.K. accents with greater accuracy. In parallel, we experiment with state-of-the-art medical large language models (like Google’s Med-PaLM 2 and OpenAI’s clinician-tuned GPT variants) to improve the quality of summaries and ensure they use the correct clinical terminology. A key research question is how to make the AI-generated notes as trustworthy as those written by a human – so we are developing techniques for uncertainty highlighting and citation of source transcript in the draft notes, so clinicians can easily review and accept them. We’re also conducting user testing with GPs to iterate on the scribe’s usability (e.g. formatting, level of detail in summaries). This R&D is keeping Vecells at the forefront of applied NLP in healthcare, and success here will greatly differentiate OneCare while meeting the NHS’s demand for reducing paperwork.

#### Scalability, Performance & Observability
In conjunction with new features, our engineering R&D is focused on ensuring OneCare performs at scale and is observable for continuous improvement. We are building a sophisticated monitoring and analytics layer to track AI decision outcomes, system response times, and user interactions in the live environment. Research is ongoing into metrics-driven feedback loops – for instance, detecting if the triage model ever flags something as routine that later is found urgent, and using that data to retrain and close the loop. We are also investing in load testing and failover designs (simulating high volumes of patient requests, sudden surges in demand, etc.) to research how the system can auto-scale and maintain performance (meeting strict Service Level Objectives like 95th percentile triage response < 2 seconds). This operational R&D ensures that as we innovate on features, we don’t compromise on reliability. It also ties into our compliance work: robust observability and incident tracking are part of the evidence needed for clinical safety cases and NHS assurance.

#### Compliance and AI Ethics Research
Given the pioneering nature of OneCare, we are also in close consultation with academic and industry experts on the regulatory front. A current research task is determining the appropriate classification of our AI under the UK’s medical device regulations – e.g. exploring whether the triage algorithm should be registered for UKCA marking and what validation that entails. We are essentially researching the “safety of AI” – documenting failure modes, designing mitigations, and ensuring our development of AI adheres to emerging best practices in ethics (transparency, bias mitigation, patient consent for AI use, etc.). We anticipate publishing or at least internally white-papering some of our findings on safe AI triage, contributing knowledge to the wider health tech community. This ongoing work will help maintain trust with NHS customers and keep Vecells ahead of any regulatory hurdles that could arise for AI-driven healthcare solutions.

In summary, Vecells maintains a strong R&D pipeline addressing both technical enhancement and safety/compliance aspects of OneCare. From improving the accuracy of emergency detection to expanding the AI scribe’s capabilities, and ensuring we meet NHS safety standards, the company is continuously investing in research that keeps the product innovative and safe. This not only strengthens our USP but is also critical for scaling in the highly regulated healthcare sector.

## 7. Business Model and Pricing

Business Model Overview
Vecells operates a B2B software-as-a-service (SaaS) model targeted at healthcare provider organizations. The primary revenue source will be subscription fees paid by GP practices, Primary Care Networks, or other healthcare providers for access to the OneCare platform. Our service provides ongoing value (continuous use of software, updates, support, hosting), making the SaaS model appropriate. This also aligns with NHS procurement preferences, which have shifted from one-off license purchases to recurring service contracts that include maintenance and support.

### Key elements of the business model: - Value Proposition to Customers: We save practices time and money by increasing efficiency and enabling them to manage more patients without proportional increase in staff. This indirectly translates to financial value (for example, if a practice can avoid hiring an extra admin or can see more patients leading to improved funding metrics). We also help them achieve targets (like extended access, patient satisfaction, etc.) which can be linked to NHS funding incentives. By framing our value in terms of both patient outcomes and operational efficiency, we make a compelling case for budget allocation. - Customer Segment and Payers: In primary care, individual practices have some budget for IT (often provided via Clinical Commissioning Groups or now Integrated Care Boards budgets for IT systems). PCNs also have development funds. In some cases, commissioners (ICBs) may centrally fund innovation for their member practices. We remain flexible: our customer might be a practice paying out of its budget or a network/ICB paying for a group of practices. We will target decision-makers such as practice partners, PCN directors, or ICB digital leads. The NHS procurement environment means sometimes the national entities might subsidize costs (e.g., NHSE might have a pot for online consultation tools that our product could fall under). We will keep aware of those mechanisms to possibly have our product covered by central programs. - Channels: Sales will be through direct engagement initially (our own sales team approaching practices/PCNs). We will also leverage referrals and success stories (a practice using it is best advertisement to neighbors). Over time, listing on frameworks (like G-Cloud, etc.) becomes a channel since procurement managers browse those for solutions. Partnerships with established EHR vendors or AHSNs can also channel sales to us (e.g., if EMIS sees value, they might recommend us as an add-on; or an AHSN might run a cohort that brings us into more sites).

Pricing Strategy
Pricing in the NHS context must be transparent, justified by value, and compatible with procurement rules. We intend to offer a subscription pricing model that is NHS procurement-fit. This likely means: - Per Practice or Per Population Pricing: We are considering a model where a practice (or network) pays based on its size (e.g., number of registered patients) or usage. One straightforward metric is a price per patient per year (since GP funding often scales by patient list size). For example, hypothetically £0.50-£1 per patient per year for the core service. A practice of 10,000 patients would then pay ~£5,000-£10,000/year. We would adjust this based on ROI calculations (if we save, say, the equivalent of 0.5 FTE staff, that’s ~£10k-£15k saved, so pricing below that ensures a net gain for the practice). - Tiered Offerings: We may have tiers, for instance: - Standard: Core triage and workflow features for a base fee. - Premium: Includes advanced modules like ambient scribe and analytics at a higher fee (some practices might opt to add those later). - Network or Enterprise: A package for a whole PCN or ICS with volume discount and extra admin dashboards. This tiering allows flexibility for smaller practices vs large groups, and upsell opportunities. - Pilot/Introductory Pricing: To encourage adoption, initially we might offer a free trial period (e.g., 3-month pilot free, then convert) or a discounted first-year price, especially for our first 10-20 customers, in exchange for feedback and case studies. NHS customers appreciate low risk trials, so this strategy can accelerate uptake. - Procurement Compatibility: We will ensure our pricing structure can fit into how NHS contracts are done. For example, it might be easier if we do an annual subscription (renewable each year, possibly 2-3 year contract) rather than monthly. NHS often prefers predictable annual costs. We’ll also align with procurement frameworks cap rules; if our annual cost is e.g. £10k, it often falls below tender thresholds individually, meaning direct award is simpler. For bigger network deals, we might go through formal tenders or frameworks.
To test pricing viability, we considered what practices currently pay: Online consultation systems historically cost in the range of £1–2 per patient per year to commissioners. We aim to be in a similar ballpark but will justify that our system does more than just online forms, so slight premium is fair. However, we also know budgets are tight, so demonstrating cost-effectiveness is crucial. We might frame it as “for the cost of a part-time admin, you get an AI system working 24/7”.
Additionally, NHS Procurement-fit entails things like: - Being on approved supplier lists. - Possibly offering data residency and termination clauses that NHS requires (like data return if contract ends). - Adhering to NHS standard pricing models (like not having hidden costs for integration – we likely bundle support and integration in the subscription).

### We will also explore alternative revenue streams in the model: - Once we have a user base, there could be an opportunity for value-added services: e.g., integration with pharmacy could allow us to get a referral fee from the pharmacy service (though in NHS context, that might not be straightforward). - Another example: If we incorporate billing for private services or elective services, maybe a transaction fee model could appear. But for now, primary focus is subscription.

Cost Structure and Margins
Our costs primarily are in R&D and service delivery (cloud hosting, support). The SaaS model typically has high gross margins once developed because each new customer adds minimal incremental cost (just some server load and support effort). In pricing, we ensure that even with conservative uptake, we cover hosting and support. For example, if a practice pays £5k/year, the cost to serve them (cloud, etc.) might be only £500/year, yielding high gross margin. Those margins go into continuing R&D and company growth.
We will maintain a lean approach where possible – e.g., using efficient cloud services that scale per use to not overspend, automating our own internal processes (like deploying updates).
Payment and Contract Terms
We’ll likely invoice annually or quarterly. Many NHS contracts pay annually in advance or per quarter. We will adapt to their preference (some CCGs/ICBs prefer upfront annual for budgeting). Ensuring we have flexible payment terms (especially in early days, maybe allowing PCNs to pay after some results, etc.) can help win trust.
Given the Innovator Founder context, we should mention our path to profitability (we detail financials in Section 10). But briefly, our business model is designed to reach profitability by scaling the number of subscriptions significantly over the fixed R&D cost base. Recurring revenue, once achieved, is reliable (NHS is a very stable payer, albeit slow to onboard).
Ensuring Fit with NHS Procurement
To explicitly address “procurement-fit” mentioned: - We will get our solution approved on GPIT Futures – that’s the NHS Digital framework for GP systems. Being on it means practices can buy us with central funding potentially. There are criteria like interoperability and security to meet, which we are already building towards. - We’ll prepare documentation needed for NHS buyers: e.g., we will have a detailed DPIA and CyberEssentials (a UK security cert) to give procurement confidence. - Pricing transparency: we’ll publish or readily provide our pricing breakdown to commissioners – no black boxes.
Finally, we will monitor policy changes. For instance, if NHS England decides to fund all practices to have an AI triage tool, we will position to be the choice. If pricing needs to adjust to fit a centrally funded model (like a cap or something), we remain flexible. The key is to get in the door and prove value; over time, with evidence, we can even argue for outcome-based pricing (e.g., if we demonstrably save X hours, perhaps share savings, but that’s speculative – core is subscription for now).
In conclusion, Vecells’ business model is a subscription service for NHS providers, priced competitively and aligned with how the NHS purchases technology. By focusing on delivering clear value (time savings, efficiency) and making it easy for customers to adopt (via appropriate pricing and procurement alignment), we aim to quickly convert interest into contracts and build a steady revenue stream to sustain and grow the business.

## 11. Legal, Compliance, and Risk Management

Operating in the healthcare sector in the UK mandates rigorous attention to legal and regulatory compliance, as well as proactive risk management, particularly given the sensitive nature of patient data and patient safety. Vecells understands that compliance is not just a box-ticking exercise but a foundational aspect of building trust with our customers (NHS bodies) and users. This section details how we address all relevant legal and regulatory requirements, and how we manage risks (technical, operational, and clinical) associated with our business.
Regulatory Compliance

### Data Protection (GDPR): OneCare will handle personal health data, which under UK GDPR is classed as sensitive personal data. We are fully committed to GDPR compliance. Practical measures include: - Obtaining patient consent for any data processing that is not strictly for direct care (in our use case, most data processing is for direct care, so the care provider’s lawful basis covers it, but for any secondary use like AI model improvement, we will anonymize or seek consent). - Implementing “privacy by design” – e.g., minimizing data retention (no longer than necessary), and pseudonymizing data for analytics. - We will draft a clear Privacy Policy for OneCare, to be approved by our Data Protection Officer (DPO). The founder will act as interim DPO (trained on GDPR basics) or we will engage an external DPO service to periodically audit us. - All staff will be trained on data privacy and confidentiality. Given the NHS DSPT (below) includes GDPR aspects, compliance with DSPT inherently means GDPR compliance too. - We’ll maintain records of processing activities (ROPA) as required and perform Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA) especially for our AI aspects, since there could be potential high risk in automated decision-making (though we keep a human in loop to mitigate that risk).

### NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT): We will complete the DSPT self-assessment in the first year, which is effectively mandatory for any IT supplier dealing with NHS patient data. The DSPT covers security policies, training, access control, incident response, etc. Actions we are taking: - Establishing an Information Security Policy and an Incident Response Plan. - Ensuring all network and system access is role-based and uses strong authentication. - Regularly updating software to patch vulnerabilities, using encryption (TLS for data in transit, AES-256 or similar for data at rest). - We will likely align with ISO 27001 standards; perhaps not certify in year 1, but by year 3, pursuing ISO 27001 certification to further demonstrate our commitment to info security (this may also be required by some NHS contracts). - Conducting penetration testing on our application (via third-party) before wider deployment – and fixing any findings. - Appointing a Caldicott Guardian or similar (often required for health data management) – our CSO or an advisor clinician can fill this role to ensure patient data is handled ethically in line with Caldicott principles.

### Clinical Safety (DCB0129 & DCB0160): - DCB0129 is the NHS standard for manufacturers of health IT systems to manage clinical risk. Vecells, as the manufacturer/developer of OneCare, will produce a Clinical Safety Case to comply with DCB0129. Our Clinical Safety Officer (CSO, Dr. [Name]) is responsible for this. It involves identifying potential hazards (e.g., “AI triage misclassifies a urgent case as routine”) and documenting mitigations (like “system has red-flag detection and if confidence low, defaults to higher urgency” or “a clinician reviews urgent categorizations”). We maintain a Hazard Log through development and into deployment, updating as needed if incidents occur or features change. The CSO will sign off that the product is safe to deploy, and we’ll provide that safety case evidence to any client who needs it. - DCB0160 is the corollary standard for healthcare organizations deploying the system. While that is on the client side, we assist our client practices by providing them with the necessary information to complete their DCB0160 responsibilities. ### Medical Device Regulations: Software that provides clinical decision support can fall under medical device regulations (MHRA in UK, under UK Medical Device Regulation post-Brexit, which is currently aligned with EU MDR for now). Since OneCare provides triage advice and could influence care decisions, we are analyzing the classification: - If it is deemed to be diagnosing or making recommendations that could affect clinical decisions, it may need to be registered as a Class IIa (or similar) medical device. - However, if it’s considered as supporting administrative workflow with clinician oversight, it might be exempt or lower risk. We are proceeding cautiously: consulting with a regulatory expert to decide if we must get a UKCA mark for OneCare. If yes, our plan (especially post-pilot) includes preparing the Technical File and engaging a conformity assessment. We anticipate by Year 2 we will either have clarified that our product is out of scope of medical devices (with documented rationale if so) or will have initiated the process for UKCA marking. Safety and quality management for this overlaps with our DCB0129 work, so we’ll likely implement an ISO 13485 aligned quality management system if needed for device compliance.

Also, changes in NHS procurement policies or funding (e.g., if NHS decides to centralize all GP digital tools procurement, it might favour bigger vendors). - Cybersecurity Incident: A major security breach or system failure causing patient harm would be a significant threat to company survival due to loss of trust and potential legal liabilities. We mitigate this strongly, but the risk can never be zero. - Customer Resistance: Some portion of clinicians or patients might resist using an AI system, reducing adoption rates. If majority of staff in a practice doesn’t engage with our tool, it could cause that site to drop the service, affecting our reputation. Cultural resistance in healthcare is a real threat. - Economic Climate: Broader economic issues (like a recession or public spending cuts) might lead to budget freezes in the NHS for new projects, making it harder to sell or sustain pricing. Additionally, tight labour markets in tech could make hiring talent challenging or expensive (though if economy downturns, sales tough, if economy overheats, hiring is tough – either scenario poses a challenge). - Intellectual Property Disputes: There’s a risk (however small) that another entity might claim our methods infringe on their IP or attempt to patent similar approaches. A legal battle could be costly and distracting. We’re mitigating by patenting and freedom-to-operate checks, but it remains a potential threat.

### Appendix D: Glossary of Terms

### AI (Artificial Intelligence): In this context, computer systems able to perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as understanding language or making decisions. OneCare uses AI for tasks like interpreting patient symptoms (triage) and summarizing consultations.

### AHSN (Academic Health Science Network): Regional networks in the NHS that support innovation uptake in healthcare. They often help pilot and spread new technologies.

### API (Application Programming Interface): A set of protocols for building and interacting with software applications. OneCare uses APIs like GP Connect to communicate with GP systems.

### DCB0129 / DCB0160: NHS Digital Clinical Safety standards. DCB0129 is for manufacturers (like us) to ensure our health IT product is safe; DCB0160 is for healthcare providers to safely deploy and use the product.

### DSPT (Data Security and Protection Toolkit): An online self-assessment tool that NHS providers and IT suppliers must complete to show they are practicing good data security and handling personal data properly.

### EHR (Electronic Health Record): Digital record system for patient health information (e.g., EMIS, SystmOne in UK primary care).

### FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources): A global standard for exchanging healthcare information electronically. We store data in FHIR format to ensure interoperability.

### GP (General Practitioner): A family doctor in the UK primary care system. GP practices are the typical clinics we serve.

### GP Connect: A programme/API by NHS that allows different IT systems to access GP records and services (appointments, patient data) in a standardized way.

### ICS (Integrated Care System): A regional partnership in England of healthcare organizations (hospitals, GPs, etc.) working together. ICSs have some responsibility for digital initiatives across their area.

### ITK3 / MESH: ITK3 is a messaging specification (“Interoperability Toolkit”) and MESH is a secure messaging service used in NHS for system-to-system communication (e.g., sending documents between sites). We use these for sending documents like triage summaries to GP systems.

### NHS (National Health Service): The publicly funded healthcare system of the UK. NHS England is the branch overseeing England’s health services.

### NHS 111: A phone and online service for urgent healthcare advice (non-emergency). We refer to it in context of out-of-hours guidance.

### PCN (Primary Care Network): Groups of GP practices working together at local level (typically covering 30-50k population) to offer broader services and extended hours.

### PHM (Population Health Management): Approach to improve healthcare outcomes by analyzing data and trends – relevant as something we might add.

### QALY (Quality-Adjusted Life Year): (Not directly mentioned but often in health economics) – a measure, but not needed in our plan specifically.

### SaaS (Software as a Service): A software distribution model in which software is hosted centrally (in the cloud) and licensed on a subscription basis. OneCare is delivered as SaaS.

### SLA (Service Level Agreement): A contract part that defines the level of service (uptime, response times) we guarantee to the client.

### SNOMED CT: A comprehensive clinical terminology used in NHS systems for coding diseases, medications, etc. We use it in our system for consistency with NHS records.

### TRL (Technology Readiness Level): A scale (1-9) to describe maturity of a technology. TRL 6-7 means a prototype demonstrated in relevant environment, TRL 9 is full deployment.

### Triage: The process of sorting patients by urgency and need, normally done by a clinician or nurse; in our case, augmented by AI.

### VIABILITY (in context of Innovator criteria): Means the business has necessary skills, knowledge, resources and market potential to succeed.

### USP (Unique Selling Proposition): The factor or benefit that differentiates our product from competitors (for us, integration + AI).

Whisper, Med-PaLM 2, BioBERT: Examples of AI models – Whisper is an open-source speech recognition model; Med-PaLM 2 is a Google medical LLM (Large Language Model) – we mention these as technology components for our scribe, reflecting modern AI tools.
